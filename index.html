Bringing Wireless Lookahead to Open-Ear Active Noise Control

Submitted to ICASSP 2027

Freddy Yifei Liu, Kuang Yuan, Justin Chan, Swarun Kumar

Carnegie Mellon University, Pittsburgh, PA, USA

Abstract

Wireless reference microphones provide acoustic lookahead that enables active noise control (ANC) systems to cancel higher-frequency and less predictable noise. However, bringing these gains to open-ear wearables such as smart glasses is challenging because they lack an error microphone for online adaptation. Instead, they rely on open-loop neural networks that must generalize across arbitrary wireless placements. Here, we present a system that brings the gains of wireless references to open-ear ANC. We first design our neural network to estimate the signal at the ear using microphones on the wearable’s frame alone. Then, this estimate and the reference signals are used to compute the control filters with a causal Wiener solve. Because the wireless microphone is not an input to the learned model, the system is invariant to its placement by construction. We show the network can be optimized end to end by backpropagating through the solve. Our evaluation shows that this approach outperforms directly predicting control filters by 9.7 dB in simulation and 5.0 dB in real-world environments.
